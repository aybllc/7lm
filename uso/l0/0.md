# L0 - meaning and claim

L0 answers two questions before any other layer is allowed to proceed: what does it mean, and what
is being claimed? It holds the ontology, the semantics, the definitions, the major claims, and the
epistemic status of those claims. Every higher layer consumes L0 by naming what it uses; nothing
higher may quietly rewrite what it finds here.

## Several meanings may coexist here

A term may carry different meanings in different sources, and L0 keeps them side by side as
source-scoped meanings rather than forcing one winner. Coexistence is permitted, expected, and
recorded. What is not permitted is downstream semantic repair: no higher layer may repair, silently
select among, or redefine an unresolved L0 meaning. A higher layer that cannot proceed on an
ambiguous term records the block and names the term; it does not resolve it on its own authority.

An unresolved L0 meaning is a legitimate, publishable state of this layer. It is a finding, not a
defect awaiting a patch from above. Resolution happens here, by the owner, at a stated scope - or it
does not happen yet.

| Term | Use in L0 | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| source-scoped meaning | a meaning that holds inside one named source at that source's stated scope, and nowhere else by default | Project rule |
| controlling use | the meaning an owner designates as controlling, for a stated scope and version | Project rule |
| canonical | owner-designated as controlling for a stated scope and version; it does not mean proven, validated, or universally true | Project rule |
| definition status | how settled a definition is, recorded per definition (provisional, source-scoped, adjudicated controlling, or superseded); a dispute ABOUT a definition is registered as a claim in epistemic/, not as a definition status | Project rule |
| epistemic status | what kind of support a claim has, recorded per claim in epistemic/ | Project rule |
| ontology | "logical structure of the terms used to describe a domain of knowledge, including both the definitions of the applicable terms and their relationships" | [External source] ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765:2017(E), Clause 3.2691 'ontology', printed page 300 |
| semantics | "semantics: The meaning of the syntactic components of a language." | [External source] ISO/IEC/IEEE 31320-2:2012(E), 3.1.175, page 21 |

## The seven L0 children

- definitions/ - the controlled vocabulary: each term, its definition, its definition status, and the
  basis on which it was written.
- epistemic/ - the epistemic status register: what kind of thing each claim is, and what would
  support or contradict it.
- ontology/ - the types, identities, and relations this corpus assumes: what kinds of things there
  are and how one is told from another.
- prior-art/ - external material bearing on L0 meaning, held as sources with their scope recorded,
  never as validation of this scaffold.
- semantic-formulas/ - formal expressions of meaning: mappings, equivalences, and the exact stated
  conditions under which two terms are treated as the same.
- semantics/ - source-scoped meanings and controlling use: what a term means in each source, and at
  which scope that meaning holds.
- thesis-statements/ - proposed, contested, or owner-designated canonical end claims, in plain
  language.

## Manual work

1. Take the routed artifact and identify every controlling term it depends on; list them before
   writing anything else.
2. For each term, check semantics/ for an existing source-scoped meaning. If one exists at a
   matching scope, cite it rather than restating it.
3. Where a term has more than one live meaning, record each with its source and scope and leave both
   live; do not pick a winner to unblock a higher layer.
4. Write or update the entry in definitions/ with a definition status and a basis, and keep the basis
   honest about whether it is a project rule or an external source.
5. Record the claim itself in thesis-statements/ and its support in epistemic/; keep the claim text
   and the status assessment in separate files.
6. If two definitions cannot both hold, do not merge them - record the disposition in epistemic/ and
   name the owner who can adjudicate.
7. Where identity or equivalence between terms is asserted, state the exact conditions in
   semantic-formulas/; similarity alone is not identity.
8. When the artifact introduces a kind of thing rather than only a word - a device, a reading, an
   occurrence - open ontology/ and write its admission and identity conditions there; when external
   work bears on it, open prior-art/ and record the source with its declared scope, its narrow
   relevance, and its negative findings.
9. Re-read every entry you changed as a consumer would: can a higher layer name it without guessing
   its scope? If not, the entry is not finished.

## Boundary

- No repair from above. L1-L6 may name an unresolved L0 meaning and stop; they may not settle it.
- Coexistence is not contradiction. Two source-scoped meanings at different scopes are not in
  conflict merely by differing.
- Mapping and similarity never establish identity or semantic authority; equivalence needs stated
  conditions and an owner.
- Formal consistency under one interpretation does not choose that interpretation.
- External vocabulary standards supply term meanings only. No external source cited here validates
  the layer arrangement of this corpus, and none may be presented as doing so.
- A **pinned** authority's content belongs to its owning repository and is never copied,
  paraphrased, or routed down from /L7/PINNED. A vocabulary source read directly and cited with its
  exact locator may be quoted or adapted, with the locator shown.
- A composition-specific or application-specific choice made higher up is not universal supersession
  and does not change an L0 entry.
