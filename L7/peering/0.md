# L7/peering/

**Object:** Directory for relationships to externally owned peers and the bounded objects exchanged with them.
**Path:** `L7/peering/` · **Layer:** L7 — SURFACE / FORWARD FACE · **Owner:** L7/

## What this directory is

Directory for relationships to externally owned peers and the bounded objects exchanged with them. It is one of the four canonical direct L7 branches: "Canonical direct L7 branches are L7/provenance/, L7/governance/, L7/peering/, and L7/publications/." The layer sheet at L7/ owns "peering: exposure/custody, ingress/egress, peer/object bindings"; this directory is where that ownership is filed.

Two independent distinctions organize the branch: "Within peering, exposure/custody (public versus private) and direction (ingress versus egress) remain independent semantic distinctions." Exposure is filed at L7/peering/public/ or L7/peering/private/; direction is filed at ingress/ or egress/ beneath each. The peer is identified at peer-1/ and each bounded object at peer-1/object-1/. "Public ingress is the natural peering position for externally owned, publicly available definitions or peer objects. Private ingress is available for controlled inbound material. Public egress handles outward public exchange toward peers; private egress supports restricted outbound exchange. Released publications are represented by the L7 publications branch, while provenance and public/legal governance remain separately addressable."

In practice a person filing here records one relation to one peer and one bounded object exchanged with it, in the position that names its exposure and its direction, using the L7 permitted actions: "receive, bind, attribute, disclose, present, publish, release, route" and "authorize, deny, quarantine, retire a binding without deleting its history". The filing passes the L7 acceptance checks: "Source, version, authority, and provenance are explicit where required." "Applicable public/legal governance is explicit where required." "External ingress is not silently admitted to L6-L0." A bound peer object is used inward only with its tag: "Every internal representation that uses it carries the exact bound version with an immutable external-ownership tag: object identity, owner, source layer, version, and ingress binding. An upstream change creates a new binding; it does not mutate the pinned object."

The Peer-object contract ("peer-1 identifies the external peer and object-1 identifies the bounded object exposed by that peer.") and the originating case ("The Autonomous/Auditonomous root issue is the originating case for the agnostic peer-1/object-1 relation.") are carried verbatim at L7/; the source side is exemplified at L7/peering/public/egress/ and the receiving side at L7/peering/public/ingress/.

## Why it exists

Preserves external ownership and authority instead of absorbing the peer into the USO. The peer stays outside the boundary; only the relation and the bound object are represented here, and "Presence on the desk establishes nothing by itself."

## Many intelligences at one face

The peering positions are not sized for one counterpart. `peer-1/` is the first of an enumeration, and the enumeration is the same on all four faces: private ingress, private egress, public ingress, public egress. So the bounded object can meet any number of intelligences at once — a person, an institution, a standards body, another repository, an archive, a model session, an orchestrator — each at its own numbered peer position, each delivery or hand-off its own `object-M/`, and none of them in another's directory. Nothing has to be added to the topology for this; it is what the enumeration already provides.

What the directory system then gives:

- **Separate incoming directories.** Each intelligence brings its material into its own `ingress/peer-N/`. Its files are bound there reference-only, NO EDIT and NO DELETE, and "External ingress is not silently admitted to L6-L0." Provenance is positional: the path says who brought a thing before anyone reads it.
- **Separate outgoing directories.** What the object hands each intelligence — a task, a definition, a question — leaves through the same peer's `egress/peer-N/`, so a hand-off and its answer are traceable by number.
- **Status one level above reach.** An intelligence writes only inside its own peer position, and only by adding a new `object-M/`. The peer's `0.md`, one directory above, is the owner's register of that peer's objects and their standing — received, under review, adopted with where the accepted content was filed, denied, quarantined, retired. The intelligence can read it and cannot write it. A bound object never moves and never changes when a decision is taken; the register records the decision, and its history stays at `USO/l6/`. That is how one party learns whether the other has processed something: the answer sits above the asker's access.
- **One acceptance path for all of them.** Whatever any intelligence brings is compared and interpreted at `USO/l5/`, and only what survives is carried inward, as the object's own authorship, citing the bound object. A person's manuscript, a body's clause, a model's draft, and an orchestrator's result take the same route. The rule is the same for a professor and a program.
- **Exposure per counterpart.** An intelligence whose relation is controlled sits under `private/`; one whose exchange is public sits under `public/`; the same intelligence may hold both, because exposure and direction stay independent.
- **Predictability for machines and people alike.** The pattern `L7/peering/{private,public}/{ingress,egress}/peer-N/object-M/` is identical in every 7LM repository. Which number is which counterpart is read once from the face's register. Nothing moves once bound, so a session, a watcher, or a colleague can be written to the path and never has to relearn it.

Described here; not instantiated by this sheet. Which counterparts are bound, at which positions, and who writes the registers is each repository's owner's decision, and the register is written by a person; nothing here presumes an automated mover. (Owner's direction, 2026-09-05.)

## Holds
- the exposure branches L7/peering/private/ and L7/peering/public/, each with an ingress/ and an egress/ position
- peer identities at */peer-1/ and bounded peer-owned or peer-directed objects at */peer-1/object-1/
- an "ingress/egress binding" with "explicit source, authority, permissions, status, and direction where applicable"
- the standing of each binding — authorized, denied, quarantined, or retired — "without deleting its history"

## Does not hold
- source identity, exact version, represented authority, and prior-art relation of a bound object — L7/provenance/
- copyright, licensing, patents, permissions, access, and restrictions attached to a bound object — L7/governance/
- the released publication object — L7/publications/
- binding history — USO/l6/; retirement or supersession of a binding — USO/l6/retired/
- comparison and interpretation of a bound definition — USO/l5/
- locally authored definitions — USO/l0/semantics/definitions/
- lower-layer scientific meaning: "L7 owns the external relation, not the lower-layer scientific meaning. Presence, publication, copyright, patent status, custody, or provenance at L7 does not make an object valid, complete, true, or internally authored."

## Boundary / traversal rule

L7 is the forward face outside the USO. It owns the external relations of the bounded object: provenance, governance, peering, and publication state. Public/private exposure and ingress/egress direction remain independent peering distinctions. L7 does not thereby acquire or change the lower-layer scientific meaning.

Standing rules that bind this branch: "A definition lives once, at its owner. L7 holds the boundary binding and provenance relation. The exact bound version may be carried inward with its immutable ownership tag, but it never becomes a copied substitute or locally authored definition." "To change an immutable external object, return to its owner. The repository using the object does not edit the peer-owned source." "Material crossing outward may lose fidelity and may never gain scientific maturity merely by being exposed."

Author note. "Public/private and ingress/egress are explicit semantic positions in the canonical research tree. Their presence does not assert that every external implementation must use the same filesystem vocabulary."
