# L7/allications/notifications/

**Object:** Directory for notices the object issues outward.
**Path:** `L7/allications/notifications/` · **Position:** L7 — SURFACE / FORWARD FACE · **Owner:** L7/

## What this directory is

A notification is an outward act in which notifying an external audience is itself the relevant event: for example, an alert, disclosure, status notice, defect notice, required report, or other notice. The representation may distinguish the notice, audience, time, and any related condition or obligation where one exists.

## Why it exists

Notification is separately addressable when the fact of notice matters independently of the broader communication content. Some notifications satisfy an obligation; others simply alert or inform. The architecture does not require an obligation for this position to be meaningful.

## Holds

- Notices issued outward, with audience or recipient, timing, and any related condition or obligation where those properties matter.

## Does not hold

- the obligation or condition itself, which is a governance fact — `L7/governance/`
- an ordinary statement to an outside party — `L7/allications/communications/`
- the writing as produced work — `L8/L4/work/writing/`
- the research meaning, argument, or claim — `L8/L5/research/`
- the internal history of drafts, versions, and supersessions — `L8/L6/history/`; retired states — `L8/L6/retired/`
- the provenance of what went out — `L7/provenance/`
- the copyright, licence, permission, access, or controlled conditions on it — `L7/governance/`
- the relationship to a named external counterpart as such — `L7/peering/` when separately represented

## Boundary / traversal rule

L7 is the forward face outside the USO. It owns the external relations of the bounded object: provenance, governance, peering, and allications — the outward acts, of which publication is one. Public/private exposure and ingress/egress direction remain independent peering distinctions. L7 does not thereby acquire or change the lower-layer scientific meaning.
