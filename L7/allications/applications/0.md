# L7/allications/applications/

**Object:** Directory for applications the bounded object makes outward.
**Path:** `L7/allications/applications/` · **Position:** L7 — SURFACE / FORWARD FACE · **Owner:** L7/

## What this directory is

An application is an outward act in which the bounded object submits a request or candidacy to an external body or process: for example, funding, admission, review, approval, participation, registration, or another externally decided request. The represented act is the application as made, including the external destination and the submitted form or version where those distinctions matter.

## Why it exists

An application is distinct from publication because its outward purpose is to request an external decision rather than simply release an object. It may also involve communication or peering, but those are separately representable relations.

## Holds

- Outward applications or submissions, with the external destination, submitted object or version, timing, and outcome where those properties matter.

## Does not hold

- the judgement about whether the application's claim is sound — `L8/L5/research/`
- a correspondence that asks for nothing — `L7/allications/communications/`
- the writing as produced work — `L8/L4/work/writing/`
- the research meaning, argument, or claim — `L8/L5/research/`
- the internal history of drafts, versions, and supersessions — `L8/L6/history/`; retired states — `L8/L6/retired/`
- the provenance of what went out — `L7/provenance/`
- the copyright, licence, permission, access, or controlled conditions on it — `L7/governance/`
- the relationship to a named external counterpart as such — `L7/peering/` when separately represented

## Boundary / traversal rule

L7 is the forward face outside the USO. It owns the external relations of the bounded object: provenance, governance, peering, and allications — the outward acts, of which publication is one. Public/private exposure and ingress/egress direction remain independent peering distinctions. L7 does not thereby acquire or change the lower-layer scientific meaning.
