# L7/

**Object:** External provenance, public/legal governance, peering, publications, exposure, and exchange.
**Path:** `L7/` · **Position:** L7 — SURFACE / FORWARD FACE · **Owner:** L7/ — this position owns the external relations of the bounded object (provenance, governance, peering, and allications — the outward acts, of which publication is one), not its lower-layer scientific meaning.

L7 is the forward face of the bounded object: the place where it meets external people, institutions, systems, repositories, legal and public conditions, publications, and data sources. The specification states the position directly: "L7 = Desktop" and "L7 is the desk: the current external surface where finished work, external bindings, provenance, governance, peering, and allications become addressable without moving their scientific meaning out of the USO." Its core question is: "What provenance, governance, peer relation, publication, or other external representation must exist at the object's forward face?"

## Position in the architecture

The following statements are quoted from or drawn from the specification's global parts and the owner's definitions, and control how this directory sits against `L8/`.

- "L0 through L6 are the seven interior dependency layers. L7 is the numbered external Surface / Forward Face; it participates in traversal but is not an eighth interior dependency layer."
- By the owner's definition `L8/L0/semantics/definitions/FIRST_IS_ALWAYS_0.md` (FIRST is 0), the seventh layer is `L6`, and 7 is the magnitude of this position, the eighth reached in traversal: numbered, not a layer.
- The name is design, not decoration. L7, pronounced El-Seven, is the desk: the uppercase L is the lower left hand corner and the 7 the upper right, the two opposite corners that frame the surface this layer is, the specification's "Surface / Forward Face", its "L7 = Desktop". The owner's words are in `L8/L6/history/specification-history.md`.
- "L7 is outside the USO and is physically a sibling boundary to L8/. L6 through L0 are the scientific interior. Physical siblinghood and semantic sequence are different relations: crossing L7 <-> L6 is a boundary traversal, not filesystem nesting; peering applies only where an external counterpart relationship is represented."
- "Canonical direct L7 branches are L7/provenance/, L7/governance/, L7/peering/, and L7/allications/."
- "Scientific construction proceeds from L0 outward only as dependencies are established. External inspection begins at L7 and proceeds inward. Description does not become prescription merely because the structure can be traversed."
- The packet reading, from the specification's clarification of 13 September 2026: "Read the bounded object as one packet. L0 is the origin: the meaning that exists in the head first and is written down second; it is not payload. L1 is the floor of the payload and L6 its end. L7 is the glue, the face at which the object binds to what is outside it. L8 envelopes them all, and its trailing edge is the connector to the next object's L0." The clause that is about this layer is the glue: L7 is where the object binds outward, and its egress is where the connector hands off. The reading changes no position and no path; `L7/` and `L8/` remain siblings at the repository root.
- One instructional peering exemplar is a semantic exchange between two bounded objects: source L0 definition → source L7 egress → receiving L7 ingress → receiving L0 use. This is one possible peering relation, not the universal route by which external definitions enter research. A definition encountered through a book, article, archive, database, standards source, or web search may be used with provenance and no peering relation at all.

### Owns / represents

- provenance: prior art, sources, lineage, authority
- public/legal governance: copyright, licensing, patents, permissions, access, controlled
- peering: public/private exposure, ingress/egress direction, peer/object relation
- allications: the outward acts, of which publication is one

### Permitted actions

- represent and distinguish external relations
- identify source and provenance relations
- state governance conditions where relevant
- identify peer counterpart, exposure, and direction
- represent outward acts and releases

### Acceptance / boundary check

- Each L7 record states only the external relation it owns.
- Peering does not silently determine authorship, acceptance, truth, or interior layer ownership.
- Public/private and ingress/egress remain independent peering coordinates.
- Publication or legal status does not strengthen scientific evidence.
- Unused canonical branches may remain empty without implying failure.

## In the case of

### Peering as an agnostic external relationship

Peering represents an identifiable relationship between the bounded object and something outside its boundary. The peer is the external counterpart. The relation may be scientific, administrative, educational, editorial, legal, computational, logistical, or otherwise external.

```text
L7/peering/
├── private/
│   ├── ingress/peer-1/object-1/
│   └── egress/peer-1/object-1/
└── public/
    ├── ingress/peer-1/object-1/
    └── egress/peer-1/object-1/
```

Public/private describe exposure. Ingress/egress describe direction. Both public and private are L7 surface channels. A private channel is still auditable to the restricted audience to which the relation is exposed; peering does not prescribe the audit regime.

`peer-N/` distinguishes one external counterpart within the local channel. `object-M/` distinguishes one object or interaction within that peer relation. The numbers do not by themselves establish rank, authority, chronology, ownership, version, status, or cross-channel identity.

Peering does not, merely by representing the relation, establish authorship, ownership, acceptance, rejection, adoption, provenance, permission, truth, scientific validity, or internal layer ownership.

[
	ext{peering}=	ext{represented relationship across the boundary}
]

[
	ext{external source}\not\Rightarrow	ext{peer}
]

A definition discovered in a book, article, archive, database, standards source, or web search may be used as an external source without a peering relation. Provenance can represent where it came from. Peering applies only when the relationship to an identifiable external counterpart is itself part of what this bounded object represents.

### Instructional exemplars

- **High-school research:** a student sends a draft to a teacher through a restricted course channel (private egress); the teacher returns comments (private ingress).
- **College laboratory:** a collaborating laboratory privately returns a calibration dataset (private ingress); a later public repository discussion can be represented separately as public peering.
- **Peer-review preparation:** manuscript submission through a restricted journal system is private egress; reviewer comments are private ingress; later publication is a separate outward act.
- **Public external interaction:** an external researcher opens a public repository issue (public ingress); a public response is public egress.
- **External source without peering:** a researcher adopts a definition found through a web search; semantic use may be L0 and source provenance may be L7/provenance, while peering remains unused.

### Originating Autonomous/Auditonomous exemplar

The Autonomous/Auditonomous case historically motivated the need to represent one bounded research object in explicit relation to another. In that exemplar, the source exposes an exact definition through public egress and the receiver represents it through public ingress. The exemplar also chooses version pinning, source tagging, and reference-only handling.

Those handling choices are valid properties of that exemplar. They do not define peering generally.

### Forward-face model

L7 is the desk: the current external surface where finished work, external bindings, provenance, governance, peering, and allications become addressable without moving their scientific meaning out of the USO.

Conceptual distinctions include consumer <-> product; customer <-> service; request <-> response; need <-> offer; incoming interaction <-> outgoing interaction; and restricted exposure <-> public exposure.

Presence on the desk establishes nothing by itself. Provenance, custody, authority, copyright, patent status, permissions, internal consistency, and publication each answer different questions; none of them alone establishes scientific truth.

### Standing rules

L7 describes external relations; it does not acquire the scientific authority of the interior merely because an object is present at the surface.

Peering describes a relationship. It does not itself prescribe ownership, authorship, immutability, acceptance, review status, handling, or lifecycle. Those properties may be represented elsewhere when they matter or may belong to a specific exemplar or implementation contract.

Public/private describe exposure. Ingress/egress describe direction. Neither pair establishes scientific validity.

An external source is not automatically a peer. Peering is used when an identifiable external counterpart relationship is itself part of the represented object.

Material crossing outward may lose fidelity and may never gain scientific maturity merely by being exposed.

An external counterpart does not acquire interior-layer authority merely by being represented at L7.

Machine-readable metadata may support automation, but the architecture does not assume that automation has been enabled or validated.

## Direction

Construction reaches L7 when work or relations become externally situated; inspection may begin at L7 and proceed inward through `L8/L6/` toward `L8/L0/`. These are the specification's two valid directions — external inspection inward L7→L6→L5→L4→L3→L2→L1→L0 and scientific construction outward L0→L1→L2→L3→L4→L5→L6→L7. Crossing between `L7/` and `L8/L6/` is a boundary traversal, not filesystem nesting; peering applies only where an external counterpart relationship is represented.
