# USO/l5/institution/peers/

**Object:** Directory for peer participant positions within the institutional setting.
**Path:** `USO/l5/institution/peers/` · **Layer:** L5 — RESEARCH / DISCOURSE · **Owner:** USO/l5/

## What this directory is

This is the directory for peer participant positions within the institutional setting. A peer position is a participant role in the research conducted at `USO/l5/research/` — a participant in review, audit, research governance, or teaching — distinguished from the research-owner role and from undergraduate positions. The position is filed here; the review, criticism, or claim the peer produces is filed at `USO/l5/research/review/` or the corresponding research directory.

In practice a person filing here records the peer position and the role it carries in review, audit, research governance, or teaching. The position gives the peer's judgment a place in the record, not evidential weight: consensus, prose, authority, or usefulness cannot substitute for proof, measurement, or a lower-layer fact.

## Why it exists

Keeps peer participation distinct from other institutional roles. A review can then be read with its author's position known and kept separate from the position of the research owner whose claim is under review.

## Holds

- peer participant positions within the institutional setting
- the role a peer position carries in review, audit, research governance, or teaching

## Does not hold

- the review a peer writes — `USO/l5/research/review/`
- claims and findings a peer contributes — `USO/l5/research/lanes/`, `USO/l5/research/findings/`
- undergraduate positions — `USO/l5/institution/undergraduates/`
- an external peer as a bound object and its ingress or egress objects (the `peer-1` / `object-1` positions) — `L7/peering/`. The specification uses the word peer for both this directory's participant positions and the L7 peering positions; this sheet states the two paths and does not adjudicate their relation.
- access relations and permissions — `L7/governance/`

## Boundary / traversal rule

L5 may interpret externally owned definitions, but interpretation does not transfer ownership of the peer definition into L0. A peer's interpretation carries the same rule.
