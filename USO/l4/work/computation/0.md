# USO/l4/work/computation/

**Object:** Directory for computation as actually run, including executable processing and recorded outputs or failures.
**Path:** `USO/l4/work/computation/` · **Layer:** L4 — LAYER 4 · **Owner:** USO/l4/

## What this directory is

"Directory for computation as actually run, including executable processing and recorded outputs or failures." Computation filed here is the run: the executed processing, its deployed bytes, and its recorded output or failure. The specification's Code case fixes the owners: "Algorithmic/engineering representation can be L3; formal proof/model L2; execution, deployed bytes, storage, interfaces, and operational result L4."

In practice, a person filing here files what was run and what came out — inputs, environment and conditions, transformations, results, tests, and rework — under pinned and resolvable lower dependencies, and records the result or failure "without converting it into a conclusion". The conversion rule names computation directly: "an intellectual object is made operative through writing, computation, experiment, integration, transport, deployment, teaching, operation, or manufacturing." The recorded output or failure is the identifiable intellectual object the layer's Output row requires.

## Why it exists

"Separates executed computation from the formal L2 relation and engineering L3 method that specify it. OSI application and network layers remain execution detail inside L4; OSI L7 is not the 7LM Surface / Peering layer."

The relation that a computation implements stays at `USO/l2/`; the method by which it approximates that relation stays at `USO/l3/`; what actually ran is here.

## Holds

- Computation as actually run: executable processing and recorded outputs or failures.
- Execution, deployed bytes, storage, interfaces, and operational result.
- Recorded outputs of a computation, including stored results. The Data case places "Collected/stored instances and transformations" at L4 without naming a child directory; this sheet does not assign them.
- A contained architecture, including an OSI stack, as actually specified, made, taught, tested, or operated — its application and network layers included, as execution detail.
- Conformance verification and rework against the governing input contract.

## Does not hold

- Algorithmic or engineering representation, precision, and method — `USO/l3/engineering-mathematics/`.
- Formal proof, model, operators, and formal encoding — `USO/l2/formal-mathematics/proofs/`, `USO/l2/formal-mathematics/operators/`, `USO/l2/formal-mathematics/relations/`.
- State categories — `USO/l1/state-space/`.
- Interpretation of a result — `USO/l5/research/`. "L4 does not authorize its own L5 interpretation."
- External source or lineage of data and code — `L7/provenance/`; internal development history — `USO/l6/history/`.
- Machine instructions for this repository. Machine-readable metadata for realized work is described at `USO/l4/` and is not instantiated; this directory holds computation as run when there is any, not automation of the scaffold.
- Writing and experiments as run — `USO/l4/work/writing/` and `USO/l4/work/experiments/`.

## Boundary / traversal rule

Writing belongs to L4 as performed work. Its research meaning belongs to L5; a released publication belongs to L7. Contained architectures may expand internally without changing their 7LM owner. When the question is how the OSI architecture operates, the complete OSI L1-L7 stack remains inside 7LM L4, and OSI L7 is not 7LM L7. When the question instead concerns OSI origins, development, justification, standardization, or design constraints, the inquiry may expand across the appropriate 7LM layers without remapping the OSI stack itself. An OSI network fault is therefore an L4 operational fault unless separately shown to affect another layer's owned object.

Contained architecture (OSI) case: "Operationally, the full OSI L1-L7 architecture remains an L4 object. Questions about its origins, development, justification, standards history, or design constraints may be railed through other 7LM layers; this analytic expansion does not remap OSI layers to 7LM layers."
