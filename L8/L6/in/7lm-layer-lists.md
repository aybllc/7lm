# The Seven-Layer Model: Layer Lists

One complete bullet list for each position of the model: the seven interior layers L0 through L6, the desk L7, and L8, the envelope that is a count and not a layer. Every bullet restates or quotes the controlling text: the layer sheets `L7/0.md`, `L8/0.md`, and `L8/L0/0.md` through `L8/L6/0.md`, and the specification at `L8/L4/work/writing/7lm-harmonized-architecture-specification.md`. Where this document and the specification differ, the specification controls.

**Standing.** Filed at `L8/L6/in/`, the inbox for L6, as a proposal: material that belongs to the layer and has not been worked into it. Whether it stays at L6 beside the field guide and the sheet templates, moves, or is withdrawn is the owner's call, HOA. Nothing in the tree, any sheet, the guide, the templates, the specification, or the CI changes with it (record: `L8/L6/history/harmonization-2026-09-04.md` §36).

**Reading rule.** Construction reads the lists bottom up, L0 to L7, and moves up only as far as the established dependencies justify. Inspection reads them top down, L7 to L0. "Description does not become prescription merely because the structure can be traversed."

**Two things hold on every list.** Every canonical branch may remain empty: presence in the agnostic topology establishes admissible capability, not mandatory population, and unused now does not mean removed. Machine metadata is described on every sheet and is not instantiated in this repository: current research practice remains manual.

## L0 — LAYER 0 · Semantic / Foundational

- **Position.** `L8/L0/`. The first position and the origin of the packet: "the meaning that exists in the head first and is written down second; it is not payload." The lowest interior layer of the USO. Band in the specification's outline: L0 SEMANTIC / FOUNDATIONAL.
- **Object.** Local semantic/rail version of the bounded thing; origin contract, not a warehouse for external definitions.
- **Owner.** `L8/L0/`, as the owner of the bounded object's locally authored foundational and rail representation.
- **Core question.** What local ontological, semantic, epistemic, and universality distinctions does THIS object establish before an admissible state is declared?
- **Function.** Hold the object's locally authored foundational representation and its exact, immutable bindings to externally authored semantic inputs, without confusing the two authorities.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: the bounded object's locally authored distinctions; no higher-layer scientific dependency. Inspection: an external definition first bound at L7 may be traced inward as an exact, immutable, externally owned semantic input to the receiving L0.
- **Output.** A local rail-grounded L0 contract sufficient for L1. A rail may remain sparse or gate-only when no local representation has been authored.
- **Hard boundary.** A source L0 definition may travel through source L7 egress and receiving L7 ingress to receiving L0. At receiving L0 it remains exact, externally authored, immutable, version-pinned, and explicitly tagged; it is not locally redefined or counted as local authorship. L0 does not own prior art, provenance, public/legal governance, state transition, operator, proof, numerical method, experiment, or research conclusion.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify the local rail or an external semantic binding: object identity, owner, source layer, version, ingress binding, immutability, gate status, and allowed actions. May enforce handling constraints; must not define or replace semantic meaning. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - Ontological, Semantic, Epistemic, and Universality rail boundaries for this object.
  - System-level traversal/attachment pedagogy as a directory/function, without making Pedagogy a fifth rail.
  - Local vocabulary or semantic representation actually authored by this object.
- **Permitted actions.**
  - bind local distinctions to their owning rail
  - state ambiguity or unresolved meaning without filling it by guess
  - version a changed local distinction rather than silently rewriting prior use
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Every L1 distinction resolves to locally authored L0 meaning or an exact named external semantic binding.
  - Every external binding retains object identity, owner, source layer, version, ingress binding, and immutable status.
  - No peer definition is locally redefined merely to make L0 look complete.
  - No neighboring rail silently supplies another rail's local meaning.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `universality/` — the Universality rail content owned by L0. Preserves Universality as its own rail instead of letting another rail silently supply it.
  - `pedagogy/` — system-level traversal and attachment pedagogy: how construction proceeds outward, how inspection proceeds inward, and how this object attaches to another by the contextual semantic route. A directory and a function, not a fifth rail. In this repository it holds the plate sheet `7lm-plate-sheet.html` and its single-source generator `7lm-plate-sheet.py`.
  - `epistemic/` — the Epistemic rail content owned by L0.
  - `ontology/` — the Ontological rail content owned by L0.
  - `semantics/` — the Semantic rail content authored by this bounded object, kept separate from externally owned definitions and later formal representation.
  - `semantics/definitions/` — definitions actually authored by this object under its Semantic rail. In this repository it holds `FIRST_IS_ALWAYS_0.md`, the owner's definition "First is always 0".
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** L0/semantics contains the semantic representation authored by the bounded thing. An externally owned definition is not copied into L0 merely to populate the directory.
- **Direction.** Construction begins here and leaves toward `L8/L1/`. Inspection enters from `L8/L1/` and ends here, where an external definition first bound at `L7/` is traced to its receiving L0.
- **Analytic view.** The L0 local semantic boundary: L0 is deliberately separated from L1. L0 establishes the local semantic/foundational contract; L1 establishes admissible states under that contract.
- **Standing notes.**
  - The four rails are Ontological, Semantic, Epistemic, and Universality. Pedagogy has a directory but is not a rail.
  - A bound external definition is referenced at L0 with its tag; it is not rewritten into `semantics/definitions/`. The binding itself and its source, version, authority, and prior-art relation are represented at `L7/peering/` and `L7/provenance/`.
  - Research hypothesis, retained open and not promoted to a rule: semantic drift is expected to originate when the local L0 meaning contract changes or is misapplied. The owner's note: "I THINK DRIFT LIVES HERE!"
  - Count rule: first is always 0. The layers are counted from 0 because 0 is the semantic origin, where the Semantic and Epistemic rails define the layers above.
  - Exemplar: the L0 object of Autonomous is its locally authored definition of Autonomous; IEEE/IEC source definitions remain externally owned inputs bound through L7 peering.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L0: the locally authored semantic distinctions required to describe what is being investigated; externally owned standards definitions stay bound through L7 rather than copied into L0.

## L1 — STATE SPACE

- **Position.** `L8/L1/`. The second position and the floor of the payload. Band: L1 STATE SPACE.
- **Object.** Admissible states and static possibility bounds.
- **Owner.** `L8/L1/` — the admissible state-space representation grounded in L0: what may exist or be represented before lawful operation is introduced.
- **Core question.** What states and distinctions are possible or admissible under the established L0 contract?
- **Function.** Freeze the board: dimensions, admissible states, invariants, exclusions, equivalence/identity conditions, and static compatibility.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: the current L0 contract. Inspection: higher-layer evidence may trigger a question or proposed revision, but cannot silently change admitted states.
- **Output.** An L1 state-space contract defining what L2 may represent and operate upon.
- **Hard boundary.** No carrying, transition, selection, learning, calculation, propagation, or transformation. Formal operation begins at L2.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify the state-space version, pinning/immutability status, admission checks, and compatibility validation. May enforce the frozen board; does not create the states or their meaning. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - admissible and excluded states
  - dimensions, domains, ranges, equivalence classes, identity conditions
  - static invariants and compatibility constraints
- **Permitted actions.**
  - admit, exclude, distinguish, bound, freeze
  - test state membership and compatibility
  - issue a new version when the possibility landscape changes
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Every admitted state is grounded in L0.
  - Invariants are mutually compatible or the collision is explicit.
  - Changes return to L1 and create a new version.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `state-space/` — admissible and excluded states, dimensions, invariants, identity conditions, and static compatibility. Establishes what may exist or be represented before lawful operation is introduced.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** L1 establishes the admissible state space. Formal operation begins at L2.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L0/` and leaves toward `L8/L2/`. Inspection enters from `L8/L2/` and proceeds toward `L8/L0/`. Higher-layer evidence may question the admitted states, but the change returns to L1 and creates a new version.
- **Analytic views.** Member of the L3 / L2 / L1 technical-possibility bundle. The tighter L3 / L2 sub-bundle applies when L1 is already pinned and not itself under question. Views, not folders.
- **State-space and claim boundaries.**
  - A valid object or transition must remain inside the declared state space and its admissibility rules.
  - An out-of-space result is either an invalid construction under the current model, or a sign that the state space is incomplete and an explicit, justified extension must be proposed. The model may not silently step outside its state space and still claim continuity with the same system.
  - Mathematical permission is not automatic scientific or engineering permission. The construction stops at the first unresolved required bridge; that is honest placement, not rejection.
  - No exotic capability gets a free explanatory advantage: FTL, unlimited power, perfect information, zero latency, infinite endurance may be studied conditionally and carry their own evidentiary and engineering burden.
  - Observable science is bounded without forbidding speculation. "Build whatever the math permits. Claim only what the layer has earned."
- **Standing notes.**
  - STATE and IMMUTABLE remain descriptive/mnemonic language; the controlling layer name is State Space.
  - The owner's word of 2026-09-20: "State Space is the key!!!" The brief for an L1 plate in three bands, how to write in state space, how to make your own, and the climb through the two gates L2 and L3 to L4, is recorded at `L8/L6/history/harmonization-2026-09-04.md` §34 and is not built.
  - Exemplar snapshot in the field guide: Auditonomous fills L1 with three files; Autonomous leaves L1 empty, its dimensions fixed inside L5 findings.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L1: admissible structural states, possibilities, exclusions, or coordinate/state-space descriptions when these are part of the question.

## L2 — FORMAL MATHEMATICS

- **Position.** `L8/L2/`. The third position; payload. Band: L2 FORMAL MATHEMATICS.
- **Object.** Exact formal objects and lawful operation over L1.
- **Owner.** `L8/L2/` — the exact formal representation of the bounded thing and the lawful operations defined over its L1 state space.
- **Core question.** What follows exactly when lawful operations are defined over L1?
- **Function.** Define notation, types, relations, operators, constructors, carriers, exact models, derivations, and proof obligations.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: the L1 state-space contract. Inspection: higher-layer work may expose a formal obligation, but the L2 object must still be stated over L1.
- **Output.** An exact L2 formal contract supplying L3 with behavior, invariants, formulas, and unresolved proof obligations.
- **Hard boundary.** No engineering tolerance, approximation quality, physical realization, measurement, or empirical truth is claimed here.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify formal artifact type, dependencies, proof/test status, domain/codomain pointers, and execution restrictions. Assists machines without replacing the formal or semantic statement. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - formal symbols and types
  - relations, functions, operators, gates, constructors, carriers, transition systems
  - axioms, definitions, lemmas, theorems, derivations, exact formulas
- **Permitted actions.**
  - formalize, derive, prove, type-check, construct, test closure
  - declare domain/codomain/partiality
  - mark conjectural or unproved claims explicitly
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Every symbol binds to L0 meaning and L1 admissibility.
  - Every operation has declared domain/codomain or partiality.
  - Exact results are supported by proof/derivation or marked as unproved.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `formal-mathematics/` — the exact formal system: symbols, types, models, derivations, and obligations, grouped under one exact owner before engineering choices are introduced.
  - `formal-mathematics/relations/` — exact relations among admitted formal objects.
  - `formal-mathematics/operators/` — exact operators, functions, gates, constructors, and other lawful formal actions.
  - `formal-mathematics/proofs/` — proofs, lemmas, derivations, and explicit unproved obligations.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** Engineering tolerance, numerical approximation, and numerical uncertainty belong to L3, not to L2 exact formal ownership.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L1/` and leaves toward `L8/L3/`. Inspection enters from `L8/L3/` and proceeds toward `L8/L1/`.
- **Analytic views.** Member of the L3 / L2 / L1 technical-possibility bundle and of the tighter L3 / L2 sub-bundle. Neither nests this layer inside another.
- **Standing notes.**
  - Current correction: "finite tolerance" is not an L2 ownership rule.
  - A mathematically valid object may stop at L2. It is honestly placed and receives no promotion by being exact.
  - A symbol that binds to no L0 meaning or no admitted L1 state is not yet an L2 object; the dependency is raised at L0 or L1, not repaired here. An exact result without proof is filed explicitly as unproved and carried as an unresolved obligation.
  - Of the split inherited term semantic-formulas, only the exact-formula part lands here; the semantic reading stays at L0 and the applied formula goes to L3.
  - Working life boundary hypothesis, untested: L2 may formalize exact relations about life, but L2 does not mathematically support life.
  - Exemplar snapshot in the field guide: both exemplar repositories keep a formulas ledger here, each currently empty, with existence and application checked in separate columns.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L2: exact formal relations, operators, proofs, or formal constraints used to state the architecture where such formalization is actually warranted.

## L3 — ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS

- **Position.** `L8/L3/`. The fourth position; payload. Band: L3 ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS.
- **Object.** Approximation, numerical representation, tolerance, uncertainty, and feasibility.
- **Owner.** `L8/L3/`, the owner of engineering approximation and feasibility applied to the L2 formal contract.
- **Core question.** How can the exact L2 relation be made feasible under declared constraints?
- **Function.** Translate formal behavior into implementable numerical/engineering methods with explicit units, precision, approximation, tolerance, uncertainty, complexity, and operating regions.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: the L2 formal contract plus declared environmental, resource, and performance constraints. Inspection: L4 evidence may force re-evaluation without silently changing L2.
- **Output.** An L3 engineering specification telling L4 what can be built/run and within what conditions.
- **Hard boundary.** L3 is not the realized L4 output and does not itself create empirical evidence.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify units, precision, approximation profile, tolerance/uncertainty bounds, implementation target, and validation conditions. Carries machine constraints while /0 carries the human description. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - algorithms and abstract data-structure choices
  - units, numerical representation, precision, approximation, discretization, tolerance
  - error/uncertainty budgets, sensitivity, stability, convergence, complexity
  - simulation, optimization, feasibility arguments
- **Permitted actions.**
  - approximate, discretize, optimize, estimate, bound error, choose representation
  - compare candidate methods to L2 invariants
  - state operating assumptions and valid region
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Error/uncertainty, units, precision, and tolerance are explicit.
  - Required L2 invariants are preserved within declared bounds.
  - Resource/complexity claims are analyzed or labeled estimates.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `engineering-mathematics/` — algorithms, numerical representation, units, precision, feasibility, stability, and complexity: the engineering translation of exact formal behavior, explicit and inspectable.
  - `engineering-mathematics/approximation/` — approximations and discretizations used to make exact relations workable; makes loss visible instead of letting it appear as exact mathematics.
  - `engineering-mathematics/uncertainty/` — uncertainty and error characterization associated with an engineering representation.
  - `engineering-mathematics/tolerances/` — declared tolerances and admissible engineering bounds, kept separate from the exact L2 relation they approximate or implement.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** L3 owns engineering approximation and feasibility; it does not own the realized L4 output.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L2/` and leaves toward `L8/L4/`. Inspection enters from `L8/L4/` and proceeds toward `L8/L2/`.
- **Analytic views.** Member of the L3 / L2 / L1 technical-possibility bundle and of the L3 / L2 sub-bundle.
- **Standing notes.**
  - Working life boundary hypothesis, untested: mathematics that depends on continuation, tolerance, uncertainty, adaptation, feedback, or environmental exchange begins at L3; L4 realizes or records the resulting conversion.
  - INFINITE is retained as a working term for open-ended tolerance/refinement across implementations and time, not as permission to move exact L2 objects here.
  - A formula divides three ways: its meaning in words is L0, the exact formula is L2, and the formula as applied, with approximations, tolerances, adaptation, and open ends, is L3.
  - The dependency test recorded at `L8/L6/history/harmonization-2026-09-04.md` §34, not written into the specification: if making the thing requires something not yet invented, and that something is not the invention itself, it does not leave L3. The whole stays; a part that requires nothing uninvented may pass to L4 on its own merits. FTL is the worked case: fully engineerable here on assumed capabilities, stopping on the drawing board because L4 cannot supply what was assumed.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L3: engineering constraints, tradeoffs, approximation, tolerances, uncertainty, feasibility, or other engineering conditions that shaped or test the architecture.

## L4 — LAYER 4 · Conversion / Realization

- **Position.** `L8/L4/`. The fifth position; payload. Band: L4 CONVERSION / REALIZATION BAY. The Harmonization Record's one sentence: "L4 is Conversion."
- **Object.** Conversion / realized work / identifiable output.
- **Owner.** `L8/L4/` owns conversion: realized work and its identifiable output or recorded failure.
- **Core question.** What conversion between intellectual property and energy, in either direction, was actually made, written, run, measured, instantiated, operated, manufactured, or recorded?
- **Function.** Own Conversion: any intellectual property to energy, or energy to intellectual property. Commit resources to realize an intellectual object, or capture an energetic event, work, or failure as an identifiable intellectual object.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: pinned L3-L0 contracts plus resources required for conversion. Inspection: realized output or failure returns evidence upward; L7 authority or release status is not a scientific construction dependency.
- **Output.** An audit-ready conversion product or recorded failure with traceable inputs, transformations, conditions, tests, results, and rework.
- **Hard boundary.** L4 does not authorize its own L5 interpretation and cannot flatten lower contracts into a convenience summary.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify build/run instructions, environment, resource requirements, inputs/outputs, integrity checks, permissions, and fault routing for realized work. Does not supply the L5 interpretation of the result. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - IP-to-energy realization: writing, models, toys, computation, code execution, experiments, integration, transport, deployment, operation, and manufacturing.
  - Energy-to-IP capture: observations, measurements, records, datasets, failures, and other identifiable intellectual objects.
  - A contained architecture, including an OSI stack, as actually specified, made, taught, tested, or operated.
- **Permitted actions.**
  - make, write, code, run, measure, test, collect, integrate, convert, store, transport, deploy, manufacture
  - verify conformance and rework against the governing input contract
  - record result or failure without converting it into a conclusion
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Governing lower dependencies are pinned and resolvable.
  - A distinguishable IP/energy conversion output or recorded failure exists.
  - Inputs, conditions, transformations, results, and evidence remain traceable.
  - L4 existence does not authorize its own L5 interpretation.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `work/` — traceable realized work governed by pinned lower-layer dependencies; one place for the performed event without flattening the L3-L0 package that governs it. A complete internal architecture, including OSI, remains an L4 object even when it has its own internal layers.
  - `work/writing/` — writing and document production as performed work, distinct from its L5 research meaning and its L7 released publication. In this repository it holds the specification, `7lm-harmonized-architecture-specification.md`, and the `.docx` generated from it.
  - `work/experiments/` — experiments as actually executed, including observations, measurements, tests, and failures; distinct from L3 measurement design and L5 interpretation.
  - `work/computation/` — computation as actually run, including executable processing and recorded outputs or failures; separate from the formal L2 relation and the engineering L3 method that specify it. OSI application and network layers remain execution detail inside L4.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** Writing belongs to L4 as performed work. Its research meaning belongs to L5; a released publication belongs to L7. Contained architectures may expand internally without changing their 7LM owner. When the question is how the OSI architecture operates, the complete OSI L1-L7 stack remains inside 7LM L4, and OSI L7 is not 7LM L7. When the question concerns OSI origins, development, justification, standardization, or design constraints, the inquiry may expand across the appropriate 7LM layers without remapping the OSI stack itself. An OSI network fault is an L4 operational fault unless separately shown to affect another layer's owned object.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L3/` and leaves toward `L8/L5/`. Inspection enters from `L8/L5/` and proceeds toward `L8/L3/`.
- **Analytic view.** The L4 realization bay: a whole realized architecture may be contained here and may expand internally according to its own native structure without commandeering 7LM layer numbers. Operational containment and analytic expansion are different relations.
- **Conversion rule.**
  - IP to energy: an intellectual object is made operative through writing, computation, experiment, integration, transport, deployment, teaching, operation, or manufacturing.
  - Energy to IP: an energetic event, operation, measurement, observation, success, or failure is captured as an identifiable intellectual object.
  - Role remains decisive: an exact model may be L2, its engineering realization may be L3, the model as made or operated is L4, and its research meaning is L5.
- **Cross-layer activity cases (placement rules on the sheet).**
  - Research: L5 interpretation/review; L6 memory; L7 external provenance and permitted presentations; L4 the research work events; lower layers their own representations.
  - Writing: the act and file production L4; the argument L5; version and correction history L6; provenance, governance, and the released publication L7.
  - Experiment: executed experiment, observation, measurement, raw work product L4; method and tolerance L3; exact relations L2; possibilities L1; interpretation L5.
  - Contained architecture (OSI): operationally one L4 object; questions about its origins railed through other layers without remapping.
  - Code: algorithmic/engineering representation L3; formal proof or model L2; execution, deployed bytes, storage, interfaces, operational result L4.
  - Data: collected and stored instances and transformations L4; representation and precision L3; formal encoding L2; state categories L1; external source and lineage L7 provenance; development history L6; interpretation L5.
  - External definition: owner external at L7 peering; source, version, authority, prior-art relation L7 provenance; legal and access conditions L7 governance; use history L6; interpretation L5; no local L0 object unless this bounded thing authors a semantic version.
  - Publication: production L4; scholarly meaning L5; internal history L6; provenance, governance, and the released publication object L7.
- **Standing notes.**
  - "THE CUT LIVES HERE." The cut is the realization boundary: L3 and below specify what is admissible, exact, or engineerable; L4 is where resources are committed and an identifiable work product, measurement, execution, or failure is produced.
  - "Intellectual property" here means ideas and intellectual objects, such as a piece of writing or a model; copyright and patents are legal conditions owned at L7.
  - A failure is a first-class L4 object, recorded, not quietly converted into a conclusion.
  - A scale, ladder, or set of status labels is an L4 object as a written thing; what it means is L5 discourse; it is never an L1 state space and never L2 mathematics.
  - Auditonomous academic-book traversal: L4 owns the Auditonomous model as made, written, instantiated, or operated, including production of the book artifact; separate layer-owned representations overlay the same bounded subject without collapsing ownership.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L4: the realized OSI architecture itself as specified, implemented, taught, tested, transmitted, or otherwise made operational.

## L5 — RESEARCH / DISCOURSE

- **Position.** `L8/L5/`. The sixth position; payload; with L6 the upper scientific interior. Band: L5 RESEARCH / DISCOURSE.
- **Object.** Interpretation, review, judgment, argument, and teaching.
- **Owner.** `L8/L5/` — research interpretation, review, judgment, argument, research governance, and teaching for the bounded scientific object.
- **Core question.** What may responsibly be concluded, questioned, taught, compared, or decided from the available work?
- **Function.** Frame questions, interpret evidence, evaluate claims, conduct review, govern research decisions, and communicate scientific meaning.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: L4 work/evidence plus relevant L3-L0 contracts. Inspection/query: questions may enter from L7; they frame inquiry but do not create lower-layer facts.
- **Output.** A bounded claim/decision with cited dependencies, limitations, review status, and reproducibility information for L6 retention and L7 presentation.
- **Hard boundary.** Consensus, prose, authority, or usefulness cannot substitute for proof, measurement, or a lower-layer fact.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify workflow state, ACL/role constraints, review status, routing, institutional access, and release readiness. Governs machine handling; does not create the research meaning. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - research questions, hypotheses, rationale, analyses, interpretations, findings
  - literature search and relevance judgment
  - claim scope, limitations, uncertainty, explanation, conclusion
  - review, audit, research governance, teaching, manuscript meaning
- **Permitted actions.**
  - compare claim to evidence; interpret; criticize; accept/reject/qualify/supersede
  - request revision from a lower owner without silently mutating it
  - prepare a claim for preservation and presentation
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Claim is no broader than evidence/maturity.
  - Methods, exclusions, uncertainty, and inferential limits are disclosed.
  - Research meaning is distinguished from L4 writing and L7 release.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `research/` — the active research record and discourse owned at L5, separate from institutional participant structure.
  - `research/lanes/` — distinct lines of inquiry within the research record, so separate questions are not collapsed into one undifferentiated stream.
  - `research/review/` — review, criticism, audit, and qualification of research claims, distinguishable from the findings being reviewed. The field guide's section 9 also names it the catch-all.
  - `research/findings/` — findings that may responsibly be stated from the available work and evidence; raw L4 output is not a conclusion.
  - `institution/` — the institutional research setting and participant positions, distinct from the research record itself.
  - `institution/peers/` — peer participant positions within the institutional setting.
  - `institution/undergraduates/` — undergraduate participant positions within the institutional setting.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** L5 may interpret externally owned definitions, but interpretation does not transfer ownership of the peer definition into L0.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L4/` and leaves toward `L8/L6/`. Inspection enters from `L8/L6/` and proceeds toward `L8/L4/`. A question that enters from `L7/` frames inquiry here but does not create a lower-layer fact. What leaves toward L6 is the bounded claim/decision; what L7 presents is that claim's release, which L5 does not own.
- **Analytic view.** The L6 / L5 upper interior: memory/history and research/discourse remain separate layer owners even when shown as one research-facing band.
- **Standing notes.**
  - A forum or chat interface may be realized operationally at L4 and exposed at L7, while the institutional roles, discourse, interpretation, review, and research meaning carried through it remain L5-owned. L7 governance concerns the external legal and access relation, not scientific judgment.
  - L5 interprets a bound peer definition under the declared semantic-deference rule: for the bounded purpose of interpreting the peer, the owner's exact bound definition is taken as authoritative for what the owner means and is not re-proved or locally redefined. This is not a claim that the definition is objectively true, not an endorsement of downstream claims, and not a transfer of authorship.
  - An argument about Autonomous cannot silently stand in for an argument about Auditonomous; a cross-object claim requires an explicit named relation or handoff.
  - A review compares the claim to its evidence and may accept, reject, qualify, or supersede it; it requests revision from a lower-layer owner without silently mutating that owner's object.
  - Exemplar snapshot in the field guide: Autonomous runs six lanes, A through F, and keeps seven findings, each carrying cited dependencies, limitations, uncertainty, review status, and reproducibility information.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L5: institutional debate, research interpretation, committee or scholarly discourse, pedagogical framing, competing explanations, and judgments about why the architecture took its form.

## L6 — LIBRARY · Memory

- **Position.** `L8/L6/`. The seventh position, the seventh layer ("layer 7 will always be layer 6 :)"), and the end of the payload. Band: L6 LIBRARY / MEMORY. The first interior layer met on inspection.
- **Object.** Memory, history, reconstruction, retirement, and query orientation.
- **Owner.** `L8/L6/` — the object's internal memory: prior internal states and versions, branch and decision history, failed paths and unresolved obligations, corrections, supersession, retirement, and retained memory.
- **Core question.** Can the object's internal history, prior states, failures, corrections, and retirement be reconstructed and correctly oriented for inspection?
- **Function.** Preserve internal memory, prior versions, branch history, corrections, failures, supersession, retirement, ledgers, and reconstructable relations without re-owning external provenance.
- **Directional inputs.** Construction: versioned artifacts and relations from L5 through L0. Inspection/query: L7 boundary events orient reconstruction; external provenance remains owned at L7.
- **Output.** A reconstructable Library record identifying the relevant prior state, branch, correction, failure, supersession, retirement, and internal relation needed for inspection or reproduction.
- **Hard boundary.** L6 records, preserves, and orients; it does not make the L5 research judgment or establish truth.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify retention, archival, retirement, supersession, reconstruction, and integrity instructions for memory/history. References to external provenance resolve to L7 rather than being re-owned by L6. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - prior internal states and versions
  - branch history and decision history
  - failed paths and unresolved obligations
  - corrections, supersession, retirement, and retained memory
- **Permitted actions.**
  - record, preserve, compare, branch, supersede, retire, reconstruct
  - retain prior classifications and corrections without erasure
  - orient inspection before entering the active payload
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Prior internal states and changes are reconstructable.
  - Correction does not require deletion of prior state.
  - External source/provenance references resolve back to L7.
  - Retirement preserves the record rather than erasing it.
- **Canonical positions beneath it.**
  - `history/` — prior states, branch history, corrections, failures, unresolved paths, and reconstructable development over time; how the object changed, not only its latest state. In this repository: `harmonization-2026-09-04.md`, the record of the layout and of every dated pass since; `specification-history.md`, the specification's versions and the owner's words behind each rule; `sheets-2026-09-07.md`, what the sheets carried about their own past.
  - `retired/` — retired or superseded internal states and bindings no longer current: retirement as preservation rather than deletion, including the history of peer bindings no longer active. In this repository: `scaffold-2026-08-20/`, `specification-draft-2026-09-03/`, `specification-2026-09-04/`.
  - `in/` — the inbox for L6: what belongs to this layer and has not been worked into it yet. Optional; a position may have one or not; it is not drawn in the specification's canonical tree and is not a position the CI requires.
- **Held at L6 in this repository as source documents an adopter takes.** `7lm-field-guide.md`, the field guide to the model; `7lm-sheet-templates.md`, the sheet templates at three degrees: minimal, standard, full.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** For an externally encountered object, L7 is the boundary and owns its external provenance/governance relation. L6 is the first interior layer used to reconstruct prior internal states, changes, failures, supersession, and retirement before deeper inspection.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L5/` and leaves toward `L7/`. Inspection enters from `L7/` and proceeds toward `L8/L5/`. Crossing L7 and L6 is a boundary/peering event, not filesystem nesting.
- **Git and scientific memory.** Git records repository change events; L6 preserves the scientific relations, decisions, failures, corrections, versions, and retirement needed for reconstruction. A commit identifier named in an L6 record is an orientation pointer; the L6 record itself is the scientific memory.
- **Standing notes.**
  - Hard rule: the guides share no history. Each repository's history is its own, kept at its own L6 and nowhere else; the source guide and every version an adopter publishes carry the model and not the past of any repository.
  - Nothing deleted until enumerated: a deletion is preceded by its enumeration in the record at `history/`; the CI does not enforce no-deletion, by the owner's word, and must not.
  - Not held here: external provenance (`L7/provenance/`); copyright, licensing, patents, permissions, access, and controlled conditions (`L7/governance/`); active peer bindings (`L7/peering/`); released publications (`L7/allications/publications/`); the research judgment about a prior state (`L8/L5/`); the current active payload of any layer.
  - Blank `0.md` and status markers in an exemplar repository remain historical implementation evidence at L6, not architecture.
  - The owner's brief for an L6 plate, a layered library showing what is where and why, is recorded at `L8/L6/history/harmonization-2026-09-04.md` §33 and is not built.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L6: reconstructable development history, versions, superseded or retired material, internal chronology, and the record needed to recover how the local inquiry developed.

## L7 — SURFACE / FORWARD FACE · The desk

- **Position.** `L7/`. The eighth position reached in traversal, numbered 7, and not an eighth interior dependency layer: 7 is the magnitude of the desk's position. The glue, the face at which the object binds to what is outside it. Outside the USO; physically a sibling of `L8/` at the repository root. "L7 = Desktop." Pronounced El-Seven: the L is the lower left corner of the desk and the 7 the upper right.
- **Object.** External provenance, public/legal governance, peering, publications, exposure, and exchange.
- **Owner.** `L7/` — the external relations of the bounded object (provenance, governance, peering, and allications, the outward acts, of which publication is one), not its lower-layer scientific meaning.
- **Core question.** What provenance, governance, peer relation, publication, or other external representation must exist at the object's forward face?
- **Function.** Provide the external Surface for provenance, prior art, public/legal governance, peering, publications, and release without absorbing external authority or granting lower-layer validity.
- **Directional inputs.** Outward release: approved L6-backed packages. Ingress: peer objects, source/authority information, and governance conditions initiate inward inspection and may carry an exact source-owned L0 definition to the receiving L0 without transferring authorship.
- **Output.** A bounded provenance record, governance record, ingress/egress binding, or publication/presentation with explicit source, authority, permissions, status, and direction where applicable.
- **Hard boundary.** L7 owns the external relation, not the lower-layer scientific meaning. Presence, publication, copyright, patent status, custody, or provenance at L7 does not make an object valid, complete, true, or internally authored.
- **Machine metadata.** May identify provenance bindings, public/legal governance, visibility, ingress/egress direction, peer/object identity, release state, permissions, and NO EDIT/NO DELETE constraints for peer-owned ingress. Does not transfer semantic authority. Not instantiated.
- **Owns / represents.**
  - provenance: prior art, sources, lineage, authority
  - public/legal governance: copyright, licensing, patents, permissions, access, controlled
  - peering: exposure/custody, ingress/egress, peer/object bindings
  - allications: the outward acts, of which publication is one
- **Permitted actions.**
  - receive, bind, attribute, disclose, present, publish, release, route
  - record provenance and prior art
  - apply public/legal permissions and restrictions
  - authorize, deny, quarantine, retire a binding without deleting its history
- **Acceptance / boundary check.**
  - Source, version, authority, and provenance are explicit where required.
  - Applicable public/legal governance is explicit where required.
  - External ingress is not silently admitted to L6-L0.
  - Publication or legal status does not strengthen scientific evidence.
  - Unused canonical branches may remain empty without implying failure.
- **Canonical positions beneath it: provenance.**
  - `provenance/` — where the object came from, what preceded it, what source/version is being used, and what authority is being represented; externally meaningful source and lineage at the forward face rather than inside internal memory.
  - `provenance/prior-art/` — identified prior art and antecedent work relevant to the bounded object; prior art is an external relation, not the object's authored L0 meaning. In this repository it holds `uha-ommp-exemplar.html`.
  - `provenance/sources/` — source identity and exact source/version references. Source files are pointed to, not copied in. In this repository it holds `source-ledger.md`.
  - `provenance/lineage/` — externally relevant lineage and derivation relationships. The owner's word, recorded at §33: lineage takes HOA input only, "that's yours".
  - `provenance/authority/` — represented source/owner authority associated with the external object or record: who speaks for an external object, distinct from what the local research concludes about it.
- **Canonical positions beneath it: governance.**
  - `governance/` — public/legal/custodial conditions attached to externally exposed or received objects, separate from provenance, peering direction, publication, and scientific judgment.
  - `governance/copyright/` — copyright-related representation when applicable. The owner's copyright rule: every copyright and licence condition is stated here once, and the licensing and controlled folders point here.
  - `governance/licensing/` — licensing terms or status when applicable.
  - `governance/patents/` — patent-related representation when applicable.
  - `governance/permissions/` — explicit permissions attached to external use, disclosure, or exchange, separate from ownership and from scientific validity.
  - `governance/access/` — access conditions applicable to externally facing objects: a boundary condition, not an internal scientific claim.
  - `governance/controlled/` — restrictions and other conditions that constrain external disclosure, use, transfer, or handling.
- **Canonical positions beneath it: peering.**
  - `peering/` — relationships to externally owned peers and the bounded objects exchanged with them; external ownership and authority are preserved, not absorbed into the USO.
  - `peering/private/` and `peering/public/` — exposure or custody, non-public and public. Independent of direction.
  - `*/ingress/` and `*/egress/` — direction, inbound and outbound, under each exposure. Independent of exposure. Public ingress is the natural position for externally owned, publicly available definitions; private ingress for controlled inbound material; public egress for outward public exchange toward peers; private egress for restricted outbound exchange.
  - `*/peer-1/` — the external peer within an ingress or egress position. Peers are counted from 1 because peers are physical. The peer's `0.md` is the owner's register of that peer's objects and their standing (authorized, denied, quarantined, retired), written by a person.
  - `*/peer-1/object-1/` — one bounded peer-owned or peer-directed object: a document, database, foreign/federated data endpoint, or repository. Peer-owned ingress is reference/binding only: NO EDIT and NO DELETE by the receiving repository. At an egress position this repository is the exposing peer.
- **Canonical positions beneath it: allications.**
  - `allications/` — the bounded object's outward acts: one family, one place, the six senses kept distinct; each a nominalized outward act that leaves an addressable record.
  - `allications/publications/` — released publication objects at the forward face, each with a release identity; writing stays L4, research meaning L5, internal history L6.
  - `allications/applications/` — applications the object makes outward: for funding, review, admission, or of its method to an outside case; an ask of a named external authority, not a release.
  - `allications/communications/` — communications issued outward: statements, correspondence, announcements, answers.
  - `allications/specifications/` — specifications issued outward for others to build or check against; a commitment to whoever builds on it, with the version they built against mattering afterwards.
  - `allications/notifications/` — notices issued outward, usually discharging an obligation; whether notice was given, and when, has one place to be answered.
  - `allications/participations/` — the object's participations in external settings: bodies, panels, working groups, collaborations, with a role and a period.
- **Boundary / traversal rule.** L7 is the forward face outside the USO. It owns the external relations of the bounded object: provenance, governance, peering, and allications. Public/private exposure and ingress/egress direction remain independent peering distinctions. L7 does not thereby acquire or change the lower-layer scientific meaning.
- **Direction.** Construction enters from `L8/L6/` as approved L6-backed packages and leaves outward toward external peers, publications, and readers. Inspection enters from the external peer, citizen, or practitioner at this face and proceeds to `L8/L6/`, and from there inward to `L8/L0/`. Crossing between L7 and L6 is a boundary/peering event, not filesystem nesting.
- **Contextual semantic route.** Source L0 definition → source L7 egress → receiving L7 ingress → receiving L0. A source object's L0 definition is an L7 egress object at its boundary; to the receiver it is L7 ingress directed to its L0. Semantic identity remains source-owned L0 throughout. L7 provenance records source, version, authority, and prior-art relation; L7 governance records public/legal/access conditions; L6 preserves binding history; L5 compares and interprets. No receiving layer thereby becomes the definition's author. Wherever the bound version is referenced internally it carries an immutable external-ownership tag: object identity, owner, source layer, version, ingress binding. An upstream change creates a new binding; it does not mutate the pinned object.
- **Standing rules.**
  - A definition lives once, at its owner. L7 holds the boundary binding and provenance relation.
  - To change an immutable external object, return to its owner. The repository using the object does not edit the peer-owned source.
  - Material crossing outward may lose fidelity and may never gain scientific maturity merely by being exposed.
  - No outsider traverses the USO by default. What an outside reader or system needs is represented at the forward face.
  - Egress is not publication: handing something to a named peer is egress; a public release with a release identity goes in `allications/publications/`.
  - Presence on the desk establishes nothing by itself. Provenance, custody, authority, copyright, patent status, permissions, internal consistency, and publication each answer different questions; none alone establishes scientific truth.
- **Standing notes.**
  - The Autonomous/Auditonomous relation is the originating boundary test of the architecture, not an optional exemplar. Autonomous freezes the narrowest reasonable agreement among its exact source-tagged standards inputs as the consensus baseline and must not manufacture consensus where sources materially disagree; Auditonomous authors its difference as an explicit local delta; baseline and delta remain separately owned and overlaid.
  - Many intelligences at one face: peer-1 opens an enumeration that is the same on all four faces, so the object can meet any number of counterparts at once, each at its own numbered position and each delivery its own object. Provenance is positional: the path says who brought a thing before anyone reads it.
  - Conceptual distinctions on the desk: consumer and product; customer and service; request and response; need and offer; incoming object and outgoing object; private custody and public exchange.
  - OSI-origin inquiry at L7: externally owned standards and source bindings, provenance, standards-publication state, public/legal governance, and relationships to standards bodies or other peers.

## L8 — The interior root · USO · the envelope (a count, not a layer)

- **Position.** `L8/`. Not a ninth position: "L8 is a count, not a position: the interior's own zero over the seven layer zeros." It names the folder that holds the interior, it counts the eight positions L0 through L7, and in the packet reading it envelopes them all, its trailing edge the connector to the next object's L0. Sibling of `L7/` at the repository root.
- **Object.** USO, the bounded scientific object's interior: the research packet on the desk.
- **Owner.** The bounded scientific object's interior; each interior layer, `L8/L0/` through `L8/L6/`, owns its own representation.
- **Holds.** `L0/` through `L6/`, physically flat siblings under `L8/`. No analytic bundle is a folder. General repository infrastructure is not automatically a layer.
- **Count rule.** L0 through L6 are the seven interior dependency layers; L7 is the numbered external Surface / Forward Face, in the traversal but not an eighth interior layer. First is always 0, so the seventh layer is L6. Byte reading: L0 through L7 are the eight bits of a byte, and L8 is the extra bit that carries the semantic, as information needs an extra bit to catch any error. The interior's zero over the layers' zeros gives 0 over 0, an 8, a closed figure.
- **Analytic views (views, not folders).** L6 / L5 upper interior; L4 realization bay; L3 / L2 / L1 technical-possibility bundle; L3 / L2 nested sub-bundle; L0 local semantic boundary.
- **Two valid directions.** Authorized external inspection inward: L7→L6→L5→L4→L3→L2→L1→L0. Scientific construction outward: L0→L1→L2→L3→L4→L5→L6→L7, only as far as established dependencies justify.
- **Architecture-wide dependency rules.**
  - Higher layers do not silently repair lower layers; a lower-layer dependency is revised explicitly, not reinterpreted in place.
  - Layer ownership is local: each representation belongs at the lowest layer where it can be stated correctly.
  - Change one layer at a time; a correction at one layer does not silently rewrite another.
  - A canonical branch may remain empty when unused.
  - Directory metadata may enforce a machine rule but does not create the human meaning carried by /0; /0 does not substitute for machine instructions.
  - Operational containment and analytic expansion are different relations; a contained architecture is not remapped because the inquiry expands.
- **Self-description.** Each local `0.md` first identifies the directory and its path, then supplies the semantic description. The two things that are not design choices: the tree the scaffold pushes, and in every folder of it a `0.md` whose first line is the folder's path from the root. Everything after the first line is a design choice; the recommendation for a layer's own sheet is at least one sentence more than the layer's number.
- **Infrastructure, not a layer.** `README.md` is the start, not the documentation: it states the reading order. `.github/workflows/integrity.yml` is the one CI check, the same in every 7LM repository: no unresolved conflict markers; a canonical root; every sheet headed by its path and every canonical position with a sheet. It moves nothing and does not enforce no-deletion. The root holds `README.md`, `L7/`, `L8/`, `.github/`, and nothing else.
