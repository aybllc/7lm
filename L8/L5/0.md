# L8/L5/

**Object:** Interpretation, review, judgment, argument, and teaching.
**Path:** `L8/L5/` · **Layer:** L5 — RESEARCH / DISCOURSE · **Owner:** L8/L5/ — this layer, as the owner of research interpretation, review, judgment, argument, research governance, and teaching for the bounded scientific object.

L5 is the layer of the USO interior that answers "What may responsibly be concluded, questioned, taught, compared, or decided from the available work?" It takes the work and evidence produced at `L8/L4/` together with the contracts pinned at `L8/L3/` through `L8/L0/`, and it produces bounded claims and decisions for retention at `L8/L6/` and presentation at `L7/`. It does not produce lower-layer facts, and nothing said here substitutes for them.

## Information sheet

| Field | Statement |

| Function | Frame questions, interpret evidence, evaluate claims, conduct review, govern research decisions, and communicate scientific meaning. |

These instructions govern machine handling; they do not create the research meaning. Not instantiated in this repository: current research practice remains manual; no directory metadata, automation, or validation is enabled here. |

### Owns / represents
- research questions, hypotheses, rationale, analyses, interpretations, findings
- literature search and relevance judgment
- claim scope, limitations, uncertainty, explanation, conclusion
- review, audit, research governance, teaching, manuscript meaning

### Permitted actions
- compare claim to evidence; interpret; criticize; accept/reject/qualify/supersede
- request revision from a lower owner without silently mutating it
- prepare a claim for preservation and presentation

### Acceptance / boundary check
- Claim is no broader than evidence/maturity.
- Methods, exclusions, uncertainty, and inferential limits are disclosed.
- Research meaning is distinguished from L4 writing and L7 release.

## In the case of

**Research note.** Forward-facing discourse forums, chat, and institutional documentation may allow fellows or participants to operate under a shared institutional identity while access controls separate participant roles and lower-layer discourse backends.

**Standing.** A forum or chat interface may be realized operationally at L4 and exposed at L7, while institutional roles, discourse, interpretation, review, and research meaning carried through it remain L5-owned. L7 governance concerns the external/public legal and access relation, not scientific judgment.

**Note.** External definitions can be interpreted here. Interpretation does not transfer ownership of the peer definition into L0.

The specification's analytic-expansion table states what an inquiry may ask or represent at this layer when a contained architecture becomes an object of inquiry. Its row for "L5 - Research / Discourse" reads: "Institutional debate, research interpretation, committee or scholarly discourse, pedagogical framing, competing explanations, and judgments about why the architecture took its form." The operating architecture under inquiry stays owned at `L8/L4/`; only the representations the inquiry produces are owned here. The specification's note on that table: "The OSI model does not become 7LM L0-L7. The question about the OSI object expands. Operational containment remains L4, while representations produced by the inquiry are owned by the 7LM layer that can state them correctly."

The specification's analytic-view table places this layer with `L8/L6/` in one research-facing band and keeps the two owners separate: "Memory/history and research/discourse are the upper scientific interior. They remain separate layer owners even when shown as one research-facing band." The note on that table adds: "These bundles are semantic and dependency views, not filesystem nesting and not mandatory traversal paths." `L8/L5/` and `L8/L6/` are siblings under `L8/`; neither contains the other.

The contextual semantic route bears on the boundary rule above. The specification's declared semantic-deference rule states: "For the bounded purpose of interpreting the peer, the receiver intentionally takes the owner's exact bound definition as authoritative for what the owner means and does not re-prove or locally redefine that meaning. This declared trust is not a claim that the definition is objectively true, not an endorsement of downstream claims, and not a transfer of authorship." L5 interpretation of a peer definition works under that rule; the bound definition itself arrives through `L7/peering/` and is owned by its source. The specification adds: "An argument about Autonomous cannot silently stand in for an argument about Auditonomous. A cross-object claim requires an explicit named relation or handoff."

## Direction

Construction enters from `L8/L4/` and leaves toward `L8/L6/`; inspection enters from `L8/L6/` and proceeds toward `L8/L4/`. These are the two valid directions the specification states — scientific construction outward L0→L1→L2→L3→L4→L5→L6→L7, and authorized external inspection inward L7→L6→L5→L4→L3→L2→L1→L0 — and a question that enters from `L7/` frames inquiry here but does not create a lower-layer fact. The specification's direction rule governs both: "Scientific construction proceeds from L0 outward only as dependencies are established. External inspection begins at L7 and proceeds inward. Description does not become prescription merely because the structure can be traversed." Construction reaches this layer only as far as the `L8/L4/` work and the lower contracts it cites justify; what leaves toward `L8/L6/` is the bounded claim/decision the information sheet describes, and what `L7/` presents is that claim's release, which this layer does not own.
