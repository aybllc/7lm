# L8/L4/work/

**Object:** Directory for realized work whose relevant conditions and dependencies can be identified.
**Path:** `L8/L4/work/` · **Layer:** L4 — LAYER 4 · **Owner:** L8/L4/

## What this directory is

This directory represents performed work: an intellectual object made operative, or a physical/observational event captured as an identifiable intellectual object. Its three children distinguish writing, experiments, and computation; a contained architecture with its own internal layers may also remain one L4 realized object when that is the role being represented.

What occurs here is the work event and its identifiable result or failure. Inputs, conditions, transformations, tests, or rework are represented when they are needed to understand the event. Relevant lower-layer dependencies remain distinguishable rather than being silently rewritten by L4. The result does not become an L5 conclusion merely because it exists.

## Why it exists

Provides one place for the performed event without flattening the lower-layer representations relevant to it. A complete internal architecture, including OSI, can remain an L4 object even when it has its own native internal layers.

The layer's hard boundary is the reason the package is not flattened: "L4 does not authorize its own L5 interpretation and cannot flatten lower contracts into a convenience summary."

## Holds

- IP-to-energy realization: writing, models, toys, computation, code execution, experiments, integration, transport, deployment, operation, and manufacturing.
- Energy-to-IP capture: observations, measurements, records, datasets, failures, and other identifiable intellectual objects.
- A contained architecture, including an OSI stack, as actually specified, made, taught, tested, or operated.
- An identifiable conversion product, event record, or recorded failure.
- Inputs, conditions, transformations, tests, or rework when those properties are part of understanding the realized event.

## Does not hold

- The research meaning, interpretation, review, or conclusion of the work — `L8/L5/research/`.
- Internal version, correction, failure, and supersession history — `L8/L6/history/`; retired states — `L8/L6/retired/`.
- The released publication relation — `L7/allications/publications/`; external provenance — `L7/provenance/`; public or legal governance — `L7/governance/`; external counterpart relationships — `L7/peering/`.
- Engineering method, tolerance, uncertainty, and approximation that specify the work — `L8/L3/engineering-mathematics/`.
- Exact relations, operators, and proofs — `L8/L2/formal-mathematics/`.
- Admissible states and possibilities — `L8/L1/state-space/`.
- Semantic distinctions and definitions used by the bounded object — `L8/L0/semantics/definitions/`.
- Generic repository support: "Infrastructure belongs at L4 only when it is the bounded work or conversion being made; generic repository support is not automatically a layer."

## Boundary / traversal rule

Writing belongs to L4 as performed work. Its research meaning belongs to L5; a released publication belongs to L7. Contained architectures may expand internally without changing their 7LM owner. When the question is how the OSI architecture operates, the complete OSI L1-L7 stack remains inside 7LM L4, and OSI L7 is not 7LM L7. When the question instead concerns OSI origins, development, justification, standardization, or design constraints, the inquiry may expand across the appropriate 7LM layers without remapping the OSI stack itself. An OSI network fault is therefore an L4 operational fault unless separately shown to affect another layer's owned object.

The cut is the realization boundary: L3 and below specify what is admissible, exact, or engineerable; L4 is where resources are committed and an identifiable work product, measurement, execution, or failure is produced.
