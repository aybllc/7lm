# L8/L2/

**Object:** Exact formal objects and lawful operation over L1.
**Path:** `L8/L2/` · **Layer:** L2 — FORMAL MATHEMATICS · **Owner:** L8/L2/ — this layer owns the exact formal representation of the bounded thing and the lawful operations defined over its L1 state space.

L2 is the layer of the scientific interior where exact formal objects are stated over the admissible states that `L8/L1/` has frozen. It asks the spec's core question: "What follows exactly when lawful operations are defined over L1?" Everything here is exact. The layer states notation, types, relations, operators, constructors, carriers, exact models, derivations, and proof obligations. It does not choose meaning (L0), admit or exclude states (L1), introduce engineering approximation, finite-precision representation, tolerance, or uncertainty (L3), or claim realized measurement or empirical work (L4). Exact mathematics may include dimensioned quantities or units; units alone do not move an exact relation to L3.

### Owns / represents
- formal symbols and types
- relations, functions, operators, gates, constructors, carriers, transition systems
- axioms, definitions, lemmas, theorems, derivations, exact formulas

### What occurs here
- formalize, derive, prove, type-check, construct, test closure
- state carriers and relation domains; for functions and operators, state domain and codomain and, when partial, the subset on which the operation is defined
- mark conjectural or unproved claims explicitly

### Boundary indicators
- Every symbol binds to L0 meaning and L1 admissibility.
- Every function or operator has a declared domain and codomain; a partial function or operator also states its domain of definition. General relations state the carrier or product over which they are defined.
- Exact results are supported by proof/derivation or marked as unproved.

## How the layer is used

The boundary indicators above describe when a representation is genuinely formal rather than merely mathematical-looking prose. Symbols resolve to the L0 and L1 distinctions on which they depend. Functions and operators state domain and codomain, with a domain of definition where partial; general relations state the formal carrier or product over which they hold. An exact claim without proof or derivation remains explicitly unproved and can carry an unresolved proof obligation. What leaves L2 is exact formal structure; later engineering treatment does not retroactively change what the exact relation is.

The interior stays physically flat: `L8/L2/` is a sibling of `L8/L1/` and `L8/L3/` under `L8/`. The spec's analytic views — the L3 / L2 / L1 technical-possibility bundle and the tighter L3 / L2 sub-bundle "when the relevant L1 state space is already established and not itself under question" — are "selectable analytic rails, not new containers and not a requirement that every inquiry traverse every layer." They do not nest this layer inside another.

Where a thing is examined rather than built, this layer owns the representation the spec assigns it in the OSI-origin inquiry table: "Exact formal relations, operators, proofs, or formal constraints used to state the architecture where such formalization is actually warranted." A contained architecture realized at `L8/L4/` is not remapped to this layer because a formal question about it arises here.

Canonical-tree rule, in this layer's words: every branch drawn in the directory structure above is an admissible capability of this layer, not a population requirement. `formal-mathematics/` and its three children may stand empty for as long as no exact object of the bounded thing has been stated; unused now does not mean removed.

## Direction

These are the spec's two valid directions — scientific construction outward L0→L1→L2→L3→L4→L5→L6→L7 "only as far as established dependencies justify," and external inspection inward L7→L6→L5→L4→L3→L2→L1→L0 — and "Description does not become prescription merely because the structure can be traversed."
