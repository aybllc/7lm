## What this directory is

Directory for the Epistemic rail content owned by L0. The Epistemic rail is one of the four rails the L0 sheet names as this object's own boundaries. What is held here is the epistemic distinction the bounded object itself establishes before an admissible state is declared at `L8/L1/`.

In practice this rail records epistemic distinctions that the bounded object actually uses before L1. A distinction may be locally formulated or adopted from an external source. Source origin does not change its L0 rail role; provenance can separately record where it came from. An epistemic distinction is not a research conclusion, evidence relation, or verdict on a claim; those belong above.

## Why it exists

Keeps epistemic distinctions inside the rail that owns them. Without this distinction, epistemic standing can be supplied silently by semantics or ontology; the L0 boundary keeps those roles separately stated.

## Holds

- Epistemic-rail distinctions used by this bounded object.
- Statements of ambiguity or unresolved meaning, recorded as unresolved.
- Versioned prior epistemic distinctions where a distinction has changed.
- Source references where useful for orientation; provenance remains separately represented at `L7/provenance/`.

## Does not hold

- External-source provenance as provenance — `L7/provenance/`. Peering applies only when an identifiable external counterpart relationship is itself represented.
- Ontological, semantic, or universality distinctions — `L8/L0/ontology/`, `L8/L0/semantics/`, `L8/L0/universality/`.
- Admissible states — `L8/L1/state-space/`.
- Research judgment, review, or findings about what is known — `L8/L5/research/`.
- Reconstructable development history and corrections beyond the versioned distinction itself — `L8/L6/history/`; superseded distinctions no longer current — `L8/L6/retired/`.

## Boundary / traversal rule

L0 owns the foundational role of this epistemic distinction. External origin does not exclude a distinction from this rail when it actually functions here; provenance and peering remain separate external-relation questions.
